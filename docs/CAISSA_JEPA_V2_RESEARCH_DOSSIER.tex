\begin{abstract}
We test whether action-conditioned predictive representations improve bounded
decisions in deterministic, alternating, fully observed, zero-sum two-player
games. Earlier controlled development studies did not establish a JEPA
advantage. The current V2.5 study tests a narrower mechanism: allocating half
of latent prediction loss to the largest error in a complete legal opponent
reply group. Strong task, scalar-consistency, reconstruction and latent
allocation controls receive matched data, architecture and tuning opportunity.
A finite-tree error bound motivates diagnostics but does not prove that the
learning procedure improves planning. This dossier separates established
engineering evidence, previous negative results, the frozen current protocol
and requirements for an independently confirmed contribution.
\end{abstract}

\textbf{Status.} Exploratory development. This is neither a submission-ready
manuscript nor a claim that the method is novel or superior. Authorship and a
publication venue have not been assigned. A Q1 venue is a quality target, not
an acceptance prediction.

\section{Question and scope}
The falsifiable project hypothesis is that predicting future latent states
conditioned on both players' actions improves planning under limited computation,
and that the benefit persists across multiple games in a defined class.
The current class has two players, alternating turns, full observation,
deterministic transitions, zero-sum utility, explicit legal actions and exact
terminal rules. Hidden information, chance, simultaneous actions and general-sum
rewards require different models and evaluation and are outside this study.

The present adapters are Connect4 on a $4\times5$ board and Reversi on a
$6\times6$ board. A single network within each run is jointly trained on both
games through common features and action conventions. This is not a held-out-game
transfer experiment. The benchmark consists of near-terminal local situations;
it is not a complete-game strength, Elo or exploitability benchmark.

The transition model receives supplied actions. It does not predict the
behavior of a named opponent. The planner minimizes over legal replies in a
finite tree; it neither averages over an uncalibrated opponent policy nor
computes a game-wide equilibrium. These objectives must remain distinct.

\section{Prior art and proposed incremental contribution}
MuZero already combines action-conditioned latent dynamics, recurrent task
predictions and planning across board games and Atari~\cite{muzero}.
SPR and EfficientZero establish predictive consistency and its use with
planning~\cite{spr,efficientzero}. TD-JEPA connects latent prediction to
temporal-difference learning and successor representations~\cite{tdjepa}.
Value-aware model learning, VaGraM and robust world-model approaches invalidate
broad novelty claims for decision-sensitive or worst-error weighting
alone~\cite{vagram,waker}. The related-work documents additionally examine
TEMPO, Minimax Model Learning, RePAIR and other board-game representations.

The distinction under investigation is the \emph{complete legal reply set for
one own action} as the unit of a fixed mean/max latent residual, together with
controls that separate latent targets from scalar targets, reconstruction and
uniform gradient scaling. The scoped search has not established exact priority.
Failure to find an identical method is not proof of uniqueness. A positive
development result would still require a defensible incremental contribution
and independent evidence; a new model name would add neither.

\section{Formal setting and conditional error bound}
Let $s$ be a complete rule state, $p(s)\in\{-1,+1\}$ the player to move,
$A(s)$ its legal actions, $T(s,a)$ the deterministic transition, and $u(s)$
terminal utility in the absolute $+1$ perspective. The encoder $e_\theta(s)$
and recurrent predictor $g_\theta(z,a)$ produce latents in $\mathbb R^d$.
For root action $a$ and legal reply $b$, define
\[
 z_s=e_\theta(s),\qquad
 u_{ab}=g_\theta(g_\theta(z_s,a),b),\qquad
 t_{ab}=e_{\bar\theta}(T(T(s,a),b)).
\]
The target encoder is an exponential moving average with coefficient $0.99$;
target branches are detached. Values use the current player-to-move perspective.
H1 therefore has the opponent's perspective; H2 returns to the root perspective.
Exact terminal overrides use the appropriate sign, including terminal H1.

For the nonterminal replies to an own action, let
\[
 \ell_{ab}=\frac{\|u_{ab}-t_{ab}\|_2^2}{d},\qquad
 J_a=\tfrac12\operatorname{mean}_b\ell_{ab}
       +\tfrac12\max_b\ell_{ab}.
\]
Use the same value head $v$ for predictions and targets. If it is
$L_v$-Lipschitz, and
$\epsilon_a=\max_b|v(t_{ab})-V^*(s_{ab})|$, then
\[
 |\widehat Q(s,a)-Q^*(s,a)|
 \le L_v\sqrt{2dJ_a}+\epsilon_a=\delta_a.
\]
The reason is that minimum is nonexpansive in the maximum norm, while
$\max_b\|u_{ab}-t_{ab}\|_2^2\le2dJ_a$. Exact terminal values enter both
backups identically. An empty nonterminal subset has zero prediction error.
Selecting the largest predicted action backup then gives
\[
 V^*(s)-Q^*(s,\widehat a)\le2\max_a\delta_a.
\]
These elementary inequalities apply to a fixed, identical, complete two-ply
tree and fixed evaluated parameters. They do not certify unseen positions,
jointly optimized encoders, equilibrium or useful learning from an average
training loss. For the tanh linear head, $L_v\le\|w_v\|_2$. Head norm and
target/oracle error are therefore measured; small latent error alone is
insufficient and the bound may be loose.

\section{Frozen V2.5 comparison}
All families use features of dimension198, a tanh encoder $198\to128\to64$,
a65-action masked policy head and a tanh scalar value head. Recurrent families
share a standard predictor $[z,a]_{129}\to50\to64$, with9764 parameters.
This architecture is a common strengthened comparator, not a novelty claim.

Each complete own-action group is sampled intact. A row receives weight $1/K$
for a group with $K$ replies, so repeated root/H1 supervision counts once per
group. Encoded policy/value supervision is shared. Recurrent families also
receive policy/value supervision at each horizon and a variance penalty.
Auxiliary losses exclude exact-terminal targets; common supervised terms retain
their original complete-group denominators. All H2 gradients propagate through
H1 and the shared predictor. Exact maximum ties share their subgradient equally.

There are42 fresh cells: seven families, two learning rates $(10^{-3},3\times10^{-4})$

equal active FLOPs, memory or wall time.

\section{Data, evaluation and decision rules}
Training uses509 roots,9237 nodes and6750 forks. Development uses209 roots
(107 Connect4,102 Reversi),3756 nodes and2735 forks. These are standalone,
fingerprinted exports from the audited self-generated bank. Original audits
check transitions, symmetry-canonical isolation and feature-byte overlap.
No external game corpus was used. Generated datasets and checkpoints remain
local; their public artifact license and large-artifact storage are unassigned.

The exact track encodes actual nonterminal H2 successors. The hybrid track uses
predicted H2 latents while retaining the same exact legal tree and terminal
overrides. Both have a4096-transition cap and one-second cooperative limit per
root. A direct model uses real successor encodings in both tracks. The primary
metric is equal-game mean action regret relative to an exact oracle, not match
win rate. Terminal overrides and rule enumeration assist every family.

One global learning rate per family is selected using three-seed mean
\emph{exact} regret; ties prefer the smaller rate. This rate is retained for
hybrid evaluation. Each track requires the candidate to improve by at least0.05
against the strongest tuned non-JEPA control, improve in both games against
every control, and improve in at least two of three paired seeds. The mechanism
also requires hybrid gains over mean/scaled latent controls and lower backed-up
oracle MSE than all non-JEPA recurrent controls. No ablation can replace the
prespecified candidate after results are seen.

Intervals use10000 paired-root bootstrap draws, stratified by game and shared
across the three fixed seeds. They do not bootstrap training seeds or correct
for adaptive reuse of development. Selection and final evaluation are separate,
unopened stages. A passed development screen is eligibility for independent
evaluation, not confirmatory significance or a publication claim.

\section{Compute interpretation and threats to validity}
Let $n$ be the number of nonterminal H2 leaves, and let $E,P,F,V$ denote batched
encoder, one-step predictor, feature and value costs. The present evaluator has
\[
 T_{\rm exact}=C_{\rm rules}+F(n)+E(n)+V(n)+H_{\rm exact},
\]
\[
 T_{\rm hybrid}=C_{\rm rules}+F(1)+E(1)+2P(n)+V(n)+H_{\rm hybrid}.
\]
The rule cost remains; H1 prediction is repeated per reply. An affine-operation
count suggests a possible crossover at $n\ge3$, but this omits batching,
allocation, nonlinearities and caching and is not a measured speedup. Actual
latency and quality must be compared against an optimized exact baseline.

Principal threats are adaptive development over the same209 roots, only three
training seeds, a narrow full-oracle endgame distribution, assistance from exact
rules, nonidentical active compute, shared project assumptions in existing
oracle implementations, and unverified unseen-game generalization. A third-party
rules compatibility audit is being prepared; it cannot establish optimal labels
or playing strength by itself. The target representation and value head jointly
evolve, so the conditional fixed-parameter bound is not a convergence theorem.

\section{Requirements for a credible next-stage contribution}
A surviving candidate needs an independently frozen selection and confirmation
protocol, replication seeds and a power-supported sample size. Held-out variants
or games, broader positions and independent reference opponents must test the
claimed scope. Same-tree comparisons and matched measured compute should be
reported separately. If scalar, decoded or scaled-uniform controls explain any
gain, the proposed latent-tail mechanism is unsupported. Negative findings and
operational failures remain visible; a final-set failure cannot be relabelled
as development without new independent data.
