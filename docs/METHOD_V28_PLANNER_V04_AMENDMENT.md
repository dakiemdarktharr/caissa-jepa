# Method V2.8-P v0.4 amendment: game scope and evidence gates

**Status: frozen candidate definition for prefit development; not novelty-certified, trained, or confirmed.** Read with planner V0.1–V0.3. This amendment replaces the 4x5 Connect4 feasibility scope with a larger board to avoid the short-game support failure. It does not relax any data or claim gate.

## Admitted game class and games

The study covers exactly two named games: deterministic gravity Connect4 6x7 (connect-four rule) and deterministic 6x6 Reversi. Both are two-player, alternating-turn, fully observable and zero-sum, with finite legal actions and explicit terminal rules. Hidden information, stochastic transitions, simultaneous actions and general-sum utility remain out of scope. The shared 8x8 padded 198-feature/65-action interface supplies board/rule descriptors; legal actions, transitions, passes and terminal utilities come only from the exact project adapter.

This pair tests two games with different transition structure; it does not establish broad cross-game transfer. A shared encoder and identical weights are required for any cross-game claim. Per-game encoders/checkpoints must be called game-specific and do not count as transfer. No external game corpus or licensed third-party labels are included. The synthetic trajectories are generated by project-owned uniform, tactical and stochastic positional policies; those are behavior sources, not optimal-play labels.

## Research estimand remains adversarial planning

The candidate predicts EMA target latents for **every legal root-action/opponent-reply branch** and uses the exact legal game adapter with a fixed two-ply max-min planner. Its opponent is modeled as a worst-case legal responder. This objective is distinct from predicting a named opponent's behavior, assuming an optimal policy learned from data, or taking an expectation under a policy distribution. Match win rate against a finite opponent suite is a separate empirical estimand and cannot be described as exploitability, minimax regret, Nash equilibrium, or opponent-response accuracy.

The primary method comparison remains reply-set JEPA versus a matched non-JEPA task-value dynamics model and direct exact-leaf value scoring under identical roots, policy/value targets, planner and measured compute. Decoded-state prediction and no-JEPA-at-inference remain ablations. The existing planner is not a game solver; it evaluates a depth-two cutoff.

## Additional prefit feasibility gate for 6x7

Before any fit, select model-blind roots from the fresh audited trajectories using the frozen trajectory/component schedule. Independently compute complete exact root action values for the predeclared confirmatory subset under a common node/time/cache budget. The gate reports all requested roots, solved/unsolved roots, action-label ties, beyond-depth roots, transitions and cost; it may not retain only solver successes while hiding failures. If exact root coverage/power is infeasible on 6x7, revise the primary estimand under a new method version (for example, to a frozen teacher-free paired-match metric) before training. Do not silently substitute partial solver labels or call behavior-policy actions optimal.

## Pass conditions and claim ceiling

The data protocol must pass schema, provenance, family-seat, overlap-component and per-split support gates. The 6x7 exact-root feasibility gate and a power analysis must then pass; the method must be independently reviewed for novelty and information/compute fairness. Only after these gates may a bounded development training pilot begin. No result supports JEPA superiority unless the predeclared JEPA arm beats the strongest matched non-JEPA baseline by the predeclared practically meaningful margin with paired uncertainty, under equal measured compute, across both games and seeds. A result limited to selected roots, one game, or a finite opponent suite narrows the claim accordingly. Q1 is an aspiration, not a predicted outcome.
