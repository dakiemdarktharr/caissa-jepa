# V2.7 prior-art re-audit — 2026-09-30

This is a targeted primary-source re-audit, not a systematic review. It revises
the novelty assessment before any V2.7 model implementation or training. Search
scope: JEPA and action-conditioned representation learning, learned board-game
planning, competitive multi-agent latent world models, minimax/value-equivalent
model learning, and game-variant transfer. The reviewed work does not establish
that the candidate in this repository is novel. Search failure is not evidence
of novelty.

| **Deep Latent Competition (DLC)**, Schwarting et al., CoRL 2020 / PMLR 2021. It makes generic joint-action-conditioned JEPA and latent imagination in multi-agent games an especially unsafe novelty claim. | **MuZero**, Schrittwieser et al., Nature 2020. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1911.08265), [official DeepMind summary](https://deepmind.google/blog/muzero-mastering-go-chess-shogi-and-atari/). | Learns latent dynamics plus reward, policy, and value signals chosen for planning, then searches in latent space. | Go, chess, shogi, and 57 Atari games; board-game evaluation reports performance matching AlphaZero on chess, shogi, and Go without supplied game rules. | **Learning to Play Two-Player Perfect-Information Games without Knowledge (Athénan)**, Cohen-Solal, JMLR 2026. [Journal page](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v27/25-2259.html), [full paper](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume27/25-2259/25-2259.pdf). | Improves learned state-evaluation functions in a minimax framework using neural tree bootstrapping from searched states, Descent (best sequences continued to terminal), richer reinforcement heuristics, completion/resolution, and an ordinal action-selection distribution. | This is the exact broad class of alternating two-player zero-sum, perfect-information games. Domains include Go, Hex, Othello, Arimaa, Connect6, Havannah and others. It compares Athénan with ExIt across 10 games, 40 repetitions per algorithm/game over 15 training days, evaluating 100 games in each seat at 2 seconds/move against base MCTS; the paper reports +62.51% ± 6.3% average performance difference vs. ExIt. In 11×11 Hex it reports 91% vs. MoHex at 2.5 sec/move over 1,000 matches. Training used a large supercomputer and the article does not establish an available software license for an implementation. | This is the closest direct game-class precedent and a strong non-JEPA method baseline. Tree bootstrapping already learns from large portions of the search tree; all-branch training alone is not novel. To isolate JEPA, compare it against Athénan-style tree-value learning and direct minimax-Q under the same search-generated states, labels, transition exposure, model budget, and measured compute. Use published results as context, not as a fair head-to-head unless methods are reproduced under matched budgets. Article text is CC BY 4.0; software/data licenses need separate checks. |
| **Minimax Strikes Back**, Cohen-Solal & Cazenave, AAMAS 2023. A JEPA study must show a marginal advantage over this value-only/tree-learning recipe, not just over a weak policy-value head. The paper is distinct from the 2026 JMLR extended treatment; avoid double-counting it as independent evidence. |

## Claim disposition

The following are **not supportable novelty claims**: first multi-game game
planner; first action-conditioned JEPA; first joint-action-conditioned latent
prediction; first competitive latent world model; first opponent-conditioned
imagination; or first minimax/model-based zero-sum learner.

The remaining candidate for further search is the conjunction of (a) training
on the complete legal counterfactual reply set at each sampled root and (b) a
separate JEPA-compatible objective that preserves the ordering of worst-case
action values, then testing whether it changes decisions under fixed search and
training-compute budgets. This is not yet distinguishable from value-equivalence,
minimax value learning, approximate state abstraction for zero-sum Markov games,
MuZero-style planning signals, or adversarial branch objectives. It is a
falsifiable question, not a method or novelty claim. The new AAAI 2025 state-
abstraction work makes “latent compression preserves minimax behavior” too broad;
Athénan makes tree-state supervision an inadequate comparator by itself; and
published cross-game policy/value transfer makes transfer alone an inadequate
increment. Before implementation, search primary sources for complete-reply-set
objectives, learned approximate Q/minimax abstractions, action-gap ranking,
value-equivalent models in turn-based games, and compute-matched counterfactual
branch prediction. If no operationally measurable difference remains, kill the
method claim and pivot to a benchmark/negative-results study rather than rename
the objective.

## Independent candidate and protocol audits — 2026-09-30

Two independent read-only reviews using the user-approved `gpt-6-luna/high`
configuration examined the complete-reply/minimax-order candidate. Both found
no support for a distinct-method claim yet. The core issue is operational: for
deterministic rules, the full two-ply closure is exactly
`{T(T(s,a),b): a in A(s), b in B(T(s,a))}`, and a direct minimax-Q/value model
trained on the same successor labels can already learn the same robust action
ordering. Adding a ranking loss does not isolate JEPA's contribution unless the
latent successor predictions demonstrate additional planning-relevant
information or a measured fixed-compute decision advantage.

The reviewers also sharpened the Athénan comparison: at each searched node the
algorithm enumerates legal children, obtains child values, and tree-bootstraps
minimax values over its partial search tree. This is not necessarily the same
as exhaustive complete two-ply closure labels at every sampled root, but it
means sibling-value/order supervision alone is already a weak novelty claim.
The AAMAS 2023 comparison reports state-data generation about 296x cheaper and
at least 7x (without reinforcement heuristic) or over 30x (with it) faster than
Polygames under its own setup; these source-reported ratios are not transferable
as local matched-compute results.

Pre-training model-blind gate, adopted from the audits:

1. Freeze terminal/sign/perspective conventions, legal-reply masks, closure
   definition, and precisely which prediction target is JEPA versus the
   minimax-order auxiliary.
2. On a predeclared development root bank, measure complete closure coverage,
   branch counts, runtime, difficult-root/timeout rates, and label provenance;
   retain all roots and censors rather than conditioning on oracle success.
3. Establish whether latent targets add planning-relevant information beyond
   direct minimax-Q labels. If no added information or plausible cost advantage
   exists, stop the JEPA-specific claim before fitting.
4. Only if gates 1–3 pass, freeze equal-state/label/update/capacity and
   equal-measured-compute tracks for JEPA, direct minimax-Q, Athénan-style
   tree-value learning, Q/minimax abstraction, shared direct policy/value
   transfer, and task/feature-prediction controls.

This gate establishes feasibility and experimental separation, not superiority
or novelty. The user goal of finding a JEPA-beats-baseline workflow remains
unmet; no V2.7 model/data/training exists.

## Stop / pivot criteria

1. If a source describes an equivalent complete-reply-set objective with
   adversarial action-order preservation, or a close method cannot be separated
   empirically from Athénan, minimax-Q, approximate Markov-game abstraction, and
   direct transfer controls, stop claiming methodological novelty; consider a
   faithful replication or a benchmark paper.
2. If teacher-label coverage is low, varies systematically by root difficulty,
   or requires post-hoc root selection, do not train the candidate. Narrow the
   domain prospectively or pivot to outcome-only evaluation with no minimax-label
   claim.
3. If a matched direct minimax-Q or Athénan-style tree-value learner reaches
   the same action ordering and decisions at equal data, updates, and compute,
   reject the JEPA mechanism claim even if the complete system plays well.
4. A positive exploratory result can only nominate a frozen candidate for
   model selection. It cannot establish cross-game superiority or equilibrium
   quality. Those require held-out games/variants, locked paired confirmation,
   and equilibrium metrics where claimed.
